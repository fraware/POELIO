# POELIO — first facilitated literary workshop

**Status:** proposed observational pilot, not a completed user study.
**Audience:** 10–25 people, ideally arranged in groups of two to four.
**Timing:** 60 minutes, facilitator-controlled; no automatic advancement.
**Materials:** public POELIO experience, one projected screen, writing materials, optionally participants' own browsers, and the printable guide at /facilitator.html.

## Objective and limits

The goal is to learn whether participants recognize the difference between a literary passage, a formal compositional mechanism, and their own interpretation—and whether the interaction invites them to invent and discuss a constraint. This exploratory workshop is not sufficient to establish causal effects on literary quality, creativity, or learning.

Do not recruit participants from the existing blinded La Relève evaluation population without reviewing possible contamination. The public workshop uses only original synthetic teaching fixtures and no unpublished manuscript.

## Choose one mechanism per workshop

**Le Registre des clefs:** an inconsistent binary eight-position schedule and exactly fourteen labelled minimum repairs. Two literary realizations are authored for the demonstration; the twelve other admissible schedules do not have prose, and the facilitator must not imply otherwise.

**Les Quatre Témoins:** four preauthored paragraphs assembled into twenty-four possible orders, with a secondary constraint preserving twelve. The software preserves the exact words of each fragment, changes only order, and never claims a particular narrative interpretation is formally proved.

The second episode is deliberately not another version of the scheduling model: permutation is a distinct mechanism with its own source unit, state space, and interpretive opportunities.

## Six-stage facilitator sequence

| Minutes | Stage | Participant activity | Facilitator responsibility |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0–8 | Read | Read silently; identify a literary detail | Do not prematurely disclose the constraint |
| 8–14 | Question | Write a first interpretation | Affirm that different readings are permissible |
| 14–24 | Explore | Change a structural condition and examine consequences | Differentiate exhaustively computed claims from guesses |
| 24–36 | Compare | Read two authorized texts or two sequences of fixed fragments | Ask what exactly changed and what stayed identical |
| 36–52 | Invent | Define a novel constraint and compose a short response | Value invention and authorial judgment, not compliance alone |
| 52–60 | Reflect | Revise the first interpretation and discuss as a group | Clarify remaining uncertainties and source attribution |

The static facilitator handout is built into the public web distribution. Participants need not register and their text is not transmitted by the browser experience; interactive drafts use per-tab session storage with an in-memory fallback, while first-reading packet responses remain memory-only; downloads are initiated explicitly by the reader.

## Preliminary observation protocol (not yet conducted)

Collect no personal information by default. If the organizers propose recordings, identifiable notes, manuscript collection, or systematic research data, first define consent, institutional review where applicable, storage, and retention.

Potential observations, with participants informed in advance, include:

- Whether they formulate a literary reading before the mechanism appears.
- Whether they can identify an exact computable property and a separate interpretive claim.
- Whether they invent a new constraint without simply reproducing the example.
- Which interactions are confusing, inaccessible, or interrupt reading.

A future comparison study requires a high-quality static explanation control, counterbalancing, independent evaluators of writing, a preregistered analysis plan where appropriate, and enough participants for its proposed inference. The pilot above alone establishes none of those causal results.

For independent first-read contacts and novice observations, use the [reader field kit](INDEPENDENT_READER_FIELD_KIT.md) and its [empty record forms](READER_FIELD_RECORDS.md). Verify manual keyboard and screen-reader access using [the human accessibility walkthrough](ACCESSIBILITY_FIELD_RUNBOOK.md). Those documents prepare a session; they do not constitute participant recruitment, consent, an accessibility certification or completed data collection.

## Operational checklist

Before admitting participants, test the public build, the printed guide, keyboard navigation, projection size, French/English interface controls, and all six stages. Confirm that no private literary catalog or reader ZIP is present in the public distribution. Pause or discontinue the session if accessibility or source-rights concerns arise.

After the session, report observed limitations clearly. Do not claim that the event represents the Oulipo or was endorsed by it.
