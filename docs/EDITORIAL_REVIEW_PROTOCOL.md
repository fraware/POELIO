# POELIO — Independent literary review packet

**Status:** reviewer-ready material. No independent reader has yet reviewed, scored, or approved these demonstrations.

## Purpose

The review concerns POELIO's governing objective: a literary encounter strong enough to hold attention before formal composition is disclosed, followed by reflection on how a constraint changes the possible work. Software functionality, mechanical correctness, and prior-art positioning are separate questions.

This protocol examines the **existing public teaching works**, not the unpublished La Relève. No private manuscript or blinded La Relève study material is involved.

## Two strictly source-derived first-reading links

Serve the public application locally using the instructions in experience/README.md. The following URL query variants expose the current reviewed **public** prose without the mathematical mechanism or a suggested literary interpretation.

Each packet displays the source verbatim via imports from the pinned public fixtures. It has no account, server-side response collection, literary scoring, or manuscript import. A participant's notes live only in browser memory and leave the browser if they voluntarily choose to export. Print with the browser's print command if a paper reading is preferable.

**Important distinction:** the page conceals the formal mechanism in its initial interface; it does not technically prevent a reviewer from examining the repository, prior publications, or JavaScript bundle. This is an *unprimed first-reading procedure*, not cryptographic blinding.

**Execution materials:** the [independent reader field kit](INDEPENDENT_READER_FIELD_KIT.md) provides an invitation that reveals no mechanism, precise sequencing for the public first-reading and companion routes, and a separate prospective novice-reader trial. The [blank field records](READER_FIELD_RECORDS.md) document critiques and permissions outside the public repository. Neither the invitation nor a first-reading URL should carry this methodological document or a technical explanation.

## Review sequence

1. Recruit a writer or editor who has not read the demonstration or the technical explanation. Disclose that they are reviewing a short fictional text; seek consent for any retained notes separately.
2. Share **one** first-reading link, without the interactive site homepage, the mathematical article, the companion, or an explanation of the procedure. Do not provide the rest of this protocol until the initial response is complete.
3. Ask them to read normally, noting imagery, diction, dramatic interest, interpretive uncertainty, and passages that resist attention. No formal-properties hint, quality scale, or preferred interpretation is supplied.
4. They may choose the page's second-pass section, which asks concrete editorial questions. Their answer is their own. They can export it locally; POELIO does not collect it.
5. Only **after** the first independent critique, disclose that the text has a rule-based construction. Offer the corresponding static companion and, separately, the interactive experience.
6. Ask whether the disclosed constraint enriches or overwhelms the original literary encounter. Document changes of opinion without attributing them automatically to a software benefit.
7. Invite proposed line edits or wholesale criticism with source passage identification. Do **not** edit pinned authored text on the basis of an anonymous impression alone. Editorial changes need a controlled version and human author approval.

For experienced Oulipian writers and formal-methods scholars, collect their perspective without interpreting enthusiasm as evidence of appeal to unfamiliar readers.

## Reviewer composition and independence

Before declaring editorial acceptance, seek at least three **independent** responses representing an experimental literary writer, a creative-writing educator/facilitator, and a digital-literature scholar or critic. These are proposed roles, not agreed participants. Record relevant expertise and prior knowledge only with consent; do not make names public without permission.

Prefer one reviewer encountering A first, another B first, and a third reading both on separate occasions. This reduces order priming in an exploratory critique, but does not create a statistically powered controlled study.

## Specific editorial questions

- Does the prose command attention independently of its mechanism? Give source-supported examples.
- Does the narrative have independent artistic stakes? If not, where is the gap?
- Which decisions of syntax, duration, imagery, character perspective, and withholding work best?
- What remains uncertain to the reader? Is that uncertainty productive or accidental?
- Does the software's later mathematical explanation illuminate a literary decision or substitute for one?
- Does the comparison of authored alternatives or reordered fragments open a genuinely different interpretation?
- What, precisely, should be revised in the prose, and what would be lost by that revision?
- Would the reviewer recommend the episode to writers, students, or ordinary readers? For which audience and why?

Do not reduce these judgments to a single aggregated numerical literary score. Record concrete reasons, tensions, and disagreements.

## Decision log template

For each response that the reviewer permits you to retain, note the date, packet version or Git commit, the work and order encountered, relevant known prior exposure, whether the formal construction had been revealed, and whether the response is recorded verbatim or paraphrased. Keep the attribution and distribution permission separate from the prose of their critique.

Classification options for an internal editor: **accept for pilot**, **revise before pilot**, **substantial reconception**, or **insufficient information**. These are editorial actions, not measures of intrinsic literary worth.
